title: A Kubecon to remember (and a flight I won't forget)
date: 2026-11-15T17:59:50.003Z
author: Sitesh Pattanaik
---
i am writing this on my way back, after having an utmost desire to fly back to seattle as soon as possible.

this is mostly due to the consequences of the FAA grounding all flights because of an issue with the ATC or something? not sure, they didn't mention it clearly either.

the unplanned stretch from friday evening till saturday evening wasn't very pleasant, as i could neither take rest nor roam around freely. the epic scene was when we got the cancellation email just 5 minutes after leaving the hotel for the airport. the timing couldn't have been more accurate than that.

but anyways, i'm not gonna crib about that for the whole blog for two reasons. 1/ this has caused a lot more inconvenience to other folks. 2/ there were a lot of things that were pretty exciting for me during the trip.

seeing such a huge venue for kubecon for the first time and getting my independent talk accepted definitely made me proud. i was even pretty elated to see the kubecon poster through the glass of the closed door on saturday evening.

the flashback of itto sushi over dinner on the first night was even crazier. during my last road trip back to seattle from new mexico with blueberry, we had stopped near downtown and had dinner at a sushi place, where we had met the exact same server. i recognized her, and she said, "i remember you guys too." that was something pretty spectacular. it's a very small world.

sunday was pretty busy with the maintainer's summit all day long, and there were some interesting things happening.

met a couple of very smart folks at google: tim hockin, benjamin elder, and also justin santa barbara. there was Alex from capital one who was pretty interesting, considering the amount of things he is doing for his project.

during a couple of sessions, folks from anthropic seemed a bit surprised that people weren't using ai, especially after hearing from tim.

then Alex, also answered a couple of interesting questions around how he was using a skill to build up context and help review the CR.

tim, the distinguished engineer from google, was pretty cool with his explanation of kubernetes, agent substrate, and all sorts of stuff during the lightning talk.

came home and got to know about eton college from blueberry. i was curious about how famous it was, and the fact that Justin had graduated from eton made it even more interesting.

exchanged a lot of ideas with jesse, micah, and martin hickey, one of the host of kubernetes maintainer's summit, at the reception.

the second day was filled with lightning talks. i was second-guessing it, but it was really nice to see the different new projects and then dive deeper if required. (pretty cool stuff.)

i was having a bit of a sore throat and was like, damn, i shouldn't fall sick now. so, i took some meds from a nearby store.

day 3, it was tuesday, and there were a lot of interesting talks happening. attended them. the next day was our talk, so i had to spend some time pretty late at night revising the topics and slides to fit the material.

then we also had the eks meetup here. raghav encouraged me and said it's pretty much a record to get two talks accepted at my first kubecon. it felt really good.

there was another invite for me from the envoy folks, and i wasn't sure if i would be able to make it, considering how the current eks one would go. but after spending some time at the eks party, i felt like i had met quite a lot of folks, so it was time for a switch.

it was cold, but i took the walk to the franklin pub, where the folks from envoy were pretty cool. we had some good chats. met another jack ma from microsoft, and it was pretty cool talking to them.

day 4, wednesday, and it was our talk. so, we met in the morning at 9:30 to practice a bit and talk through it. it was really good, and that really helped us with the flow on the event day.

ankur, akarsh, and i were hanging around, and that's when he talked about sugar, the gcm patch monitors, and the importance of walking, tracking your health, and all sorts of things that would matter in the long run to avoid diseases like diabetes.

it was pretty scary, and i was curious to know what happens to my body. hence, by the end of the trip, i had ordered one that he recommended using my pending fsa.

wednesday night was pretty busy, and then i realized that i hadn't completed my slides. so, i had to work on my slides, video edits, and results from kiro. all of that still took quite some time, until around 2:30 am.

i was of the opinion that i should at least practice and make sure that i make a single pass before the next day's talk, which was at 11. that way, even if i woke up a bit late, it should be fine.

woke up at the normal time and then wanted to explore some quick bites. but while exploring the different cafes nearby, i felt that almost half the people staying at the hotels were in the cafes, with decent queues. so, we went back to the starbucks in the hotel. one of the worst coffees i have ever had.

took some meds and headed back to 255 bc for my talk. arrived a bit early and made sure that the font size and everything else were visible from the back. an envoy maintainer handed me a cap. that was nice.

finished my talk, and puff, it was a good one! even though i had to rush through towards the end, it felt good to have a live demo.

collected goodies like it was a college hackathon. filled my bag with all kinds of things: t-shirts, stickers, and anything and everything that i could pick up.

i was a free bird and then came back home to prepare for the evening gathering at 6, the tapas-style dinner.

it was a really good evening. had a pretty deep conversation with laura, jesse, stephen, and daniel. the food was good, and the conversations were even better. loved each part of it, and that $140 was worth the value to get closer to some of these very talented folks.

headed back home, took some meds, and rested for the day.

the next day was friday, and we had to leave. my colleagues wanted to visit downtown, so we roamed around a bit, visited the mormon temple and the utah capitol, and had sushi before finishing up my last set of meetings and heading to the airport.

then got to know about the famous FAA plane grounding that was impacting everything.

we waited and watched as the ETA kept moving forward, until eventually the flight was cancelled right when we were on the uber ride to the airport. we asked the uber driver to drop us back at the hotel for convenience.

had a casual chat around and booked the next day's flight.

then saturday, it was quite chill. we woke up, had some breakfast, and completed some pending work before checking out of the hotel.

got lunch, grabbed some coffee at cupla coffee house, and headed to the airport.

this time, though, we were finally able to board the flight, and it departed on time.

and with that, my first kubecon north america trip came to an end.
